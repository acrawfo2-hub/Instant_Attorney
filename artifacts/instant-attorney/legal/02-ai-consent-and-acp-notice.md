## Part A — Consent to AI-Assisted Services

### A.1 How AI is used
The Firm uses artificial intelligence tools, including large language models
provided by **Anthropic, PBC (Claude)** and, when you select it in account
settings, **xAI (Grok)**, to assist with: (a) intake and fact-gathering;
(b) analysis of legal issues; and (c) drafting of legal documents. Some features
may continue to use Anthropic even if you prefer Grok. AI accelerates and
supports the work; **it does not replace the judgment of a licensed attorney
and is not your attorney.**

### A.2 Attorney review and supervision
A licensed attorney supervises the use of AI and **reviews AI-generated documents
before they are delivered to you as attorney-approved.** Drafts presented before
attorney approval are labeled as pre-review and must not be filed, signed, or
relied upon. These practices are intended to satisfy the Firm's duties of
competence, diligence, communication, confidentiality, and candor under the Texas
Disciplinary Rules of Professional Conduct and **Texas Ethics Opinion 705**.

### A.3 Limitations of AI
You understand that AI systems can produce output that is incomplete, outdated, or
incorrect (including "hallucinations"), and that AI does not guarantee accuracy.
The attorney review step is intended to address this, but no system is perfect. You
should raise any concern about a document with the Firm before acting on it.

### A.4 Confidentiality and data handling
The Firm treats the information you provide in Phase II as confidential client
information under Tex. Disciplinary R. 1.05. The Firm uses **AI providers under
confidentiality and data-processing obligations** — **Anthropic, PBC** by default,
and **xAI** when you select Grok or when a feature requires Anthropic-only
capabilities — **does not permit your communications to be used to train AI
models**, and is implementing **zero-data-retention (ZDR) processing with each AI
provider before that provider is used for public client content** so that your
prompt and output content is not retained by that provider. See the Privacy Policy
(Document 4) for full detail.

### A.5 Consent to third-party service providers
You consent to processing of your information by the categories of service
providers the Firm uses to deliver the service, including: **AI model providers**
(inference), **cloud hosting and storage providers**, **payment processors**, and
**transactional email providers**. These providers act as the Firm's service
providers/agents under contractual confidentiality and data-protection
obligations. Current vendor categories and identities are described in the Privacy
Policy.

### A.6 Billing for AI usage
You acknowledge that AI usage has a cost and that the Firm charges for it through
the subscription and automatic usage top-ups described in your Representation
Agreement (Section 4) and the Billing and Refund Disclosure (Document 5).
Different models have different underlying costs; the Firm meters usage in
dollars of model cost, so a lower-cost model preference can reduce how quickly
usage top-ups are triggered.

### A.7 Revocation
You may revoke this consent at any time by contacting the Firm in writing. Because
AI assistance is integral to how the Platform delivers services, revoking your
consent will generally mean the Firm can no longer provide Platform services to
you, and the engagement may be terminated. Revocation does not affect the
lawfulness of processing that occurred before revocation, and does not undo work
already performed or charges already incurred.

## Part B — Attorney-Client Privilege (ACP) Notice and Limits

**Please read this carefully. Privilege is powerful but it is not unlimited.**

### B.1 When privilege applies
- **Phase I (free) communications are NOT privileged** and do not create an
  attorney-client relationship. Do **not** share confidential or sensitive facts in
  Phase I.
- **Phase II communications** made to obtain legal services, after you sign the
  Representation Agreement and while your subscription is active, are intended to be
  protected by attorney-client privilege and the duty of confidentiality.

### B.2 Important limits on privilege
Privilege may be lost or may not apply in situations including:
- **Crime-fraud exception:** Communications made to obtain help committing or
  furthering a crime or fraud, or to plan future unlawful conduct, are **not**
  privileged.
- **Third-party disclosure:** If you share privileged communications with people
  outside the attorney-client relationship, privilege may be waived.
- **Your own disclosure or use:** Posting, forwarding, or publicly using the
  communications can waive privilege.
- **Court determination:** Privilege is applied by courts under applicable law. The
  Firm cannot guarantee that any particular court or tribunal will recognize
  privilege over any particular communication.

### B.3 Use of vendors does not, by itself, waive privilege
The Firm uses service providers (such as AI and cloud vendors) as its agents under
confidentiality obligations to deliver legal services. Using such agents is not
intended to, and is generally understood not to, waive privilege — but see B.2.

### B.4 Practical guidance
To protect privilege: keep your account credentials secure; do not share your
Phase II conversations or documents with third parties; and use the Platform only
to seek legal guidance for lawful purposes.
